Pay cash, or finance and hold the money at 4%?

The rule: finance by default, but only where investing the cash actually offsets the loan — i.e. only when the loan’s effective APR is below your after-tax yield. A 4% CD at a 24% federal + 9.3% California marginal rate nets 2.67%, and almost nothing on offer is cheaper than that.

It clears on the CX-50. It does not clear on the RAV4 — and the RAV4 is the recommendation. Toyota is running no rebate and no subsidised rate on the redesign, so 4.99% is a real 4.99% against a 2.67% yield. No outside lender closes that gap either; credit-union new-car money is still 5%+. Financing it anyway costs about $2,121 over four years, which is the price of keeping $40k liquid — a fair trade if you want the optionality, but it is not arbitrage.

Break-even is a 2.33% after-tax yield — at 4% gross, that means a combined marginal rate under 41.6%.

Use Treasuries, not a CD. Treasury interest is exempt from state income tax, so 4% nets 3.04% instead of 2.67% in California. Same risk, same liquidity, and it doubles the financing gain to $481. There is no reason to hold a CD in a 9.3% state.

And lock the term. If the 4% is a floating money-market yield rather than a locked CD, a Fed cut puts you underwater by month 20. Match the CD or T-bill ladder to the loan term.

The RAV4 is the one to be skeptical about. San Diego CarGurus inventory averages $43,185 with only 36% of listings rated a good or great deal — the worst share in this set. Documented California markups run $3,999–$5,995, though every confirmed case is Northern California. Paying straight MSRP would be a win here. Walk at $39,000+.

The CX-50 is the opposite — but its margin is thinner than it looks. Invoice is $38,691 delivered, only $954 under MSRP, plus 2–3% holdback. That is roughly $1,700–2,100 of total dealer room, so a discount past ~$1,250 is a real fight. With the $1,500 cash on top, $37,000 is a good outcome.

Mazda runs customer cash on the CX-50 most months. If the July program lapses, wait for the August one rather than paying to beat a deadline — the rebate is worth more than any urgency premium a dealer will extract in the last three days of a month.

California caps dealer doc fees at $85 by statute (Vehicle Code §11713.1) — a higher doc fee is not compliant. Add ~$620 DMV/registration/VLF and 7.75% tax; multiply the negotiated price by roughly 1.096 for out-the-door. Anything labelled “market adjustment”, dealer prep, nitrogen or VIN etch is negotiable or refusable.

The Equinox’s 0% is the only genuinely free money in this whole analysis. GM is running no cash allowance nationally, so unlike Mazda’s there is no rebate to give up — it is a true zero. Worth $1,858. Take it if he buys the car. It still isn’t enough to move the Equinox off last place.

Note the residency wrinkle: living in Michigan year-round while domiciled in Tennessee changes the state tax on that interest. Michigan’s 4.25% flat tax would cut the after-tax yield to 2.87% and shave roughly $120 off each financing gain above. It does not flip any verdict.

Pay cash for the RAV4. Toyota is offering no rebate and no subsidised rate on the redesign, so its 4.99% is a real 4.99% — well above a 3.04% after-tax yield. Financing it costs $1,800 over four years.

The Accord shrinks the arbitrage. Every gain above is computed with no trade applied. A $20,000 Accord cuts each loan roughly in half, and the investment gain falls with it — the Equinox’s $1,858 drops to about $622.

That flips one call outright. Mazda’s 0% costs a fixed $1,500 in forfeited rebate no matter how small the loan gets, so once the Accord is worth about $9,000 or more, the CX-50’s 0% stops paying for itself and cash wins. The Equinox’s 0% never flips, because it forfeits nothing — it just earns less. Use the slider on the Best-deal tab to see it move.

Tires — still the highest-return decision on the list

Michelin CrossClimate2, 3PMSF-rated, 225/65R17 — roughly $900–1,000 installed. One set, year-round. Conventional all-seasons lose grip below about 45°F no matter how many wheels are driven; a front-drive car on winter-rated rubber beats an AWD car on all-seasons. Negotiate a tire credit at delivery or sell the factory takeoffs for $400–600. Avoid the Equinox ACTIV — its all-terrain tires are worse on ice and noisier.

Verify before signing

Every item below is either unconfirmed or time-sensitive.

  • timingMazda’s 0% APR and $1,500 cash are posted through July 31, 2026. Mazda runs some version of both most months, so a lapse is not a reason to rush — wait for the August program rather than paying an end-of-month premium to beat a deadline. Confirm the current terms before planning around either. They remain strictly either/or.
  • blockerAny used Equinox must be VIN-checked for Convenience Package II. Without it the driver’s seat is a 6-way manual, which fails a hard requirement. Most used LT stock is ex-rental and will not have it.
  • unverifiedThe GM employee price for a 2026 Equinox LT AWD could not be confirmed by any public source. The ~$31,600 figure is an assumption. So is whether it stacks with the current 0%/36mo offer, which requires delivery by August 3.
  • confirmMazda’s longer-term rates are set regionally — 1.9%, 2.9% and 3.9% at 60 months all appear in different markets. Call a San Diego dealer for the actual ladder.
  • confirmThe $500 loyalty and $500 military rebates are reported to stack with special APR, but this is not confirmed in Mazda’s written terms.
  • noteThe Equinox’s $945 Safety & Technology package is optional — blind-spot, rear cross-traffic braking and reverse automatic braking are already standard on LT. The package buys surround-view cameras, a camera mirror and fog lamps.
  • note2027 Equinox stock has arrived, so any 2026 on a lot is leftover inventory. One dealer was asking $3,300 under sticker.
  • noteAny CX-50 Premium quote above $39,645 means premium paint ($595) or dealer add-ons. Demand itemization.
  • noteBuy GAP coverage from an insurer or credit union, never from the finance office.
  • noteSit in the car 20+ minutes before deciding — Consumer Reports flagged the CR-V’s front seats as firm.
